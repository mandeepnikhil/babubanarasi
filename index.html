A note from Babu

Come, sit with us a while.

In Banaras, nothing is rushed. A saree takes weeks on the loom. A morning on the Ganga starts before the sun does. Chai is poured slowly and drunk slower still.

We started Babu Banarasi to share that unhurried city with the world: the silk our weavers make by hand, the ghats we grew up walking, the food from our own fields. Everything here comes straight from the people who make it, and we vouch for every piece and every experience as we would for family.

A day in Kashi

From the first bell
to the last paan.

Banaras keeps its own clock. Here is a day as we'd spend it with you, hour by hour.

4:45 am

  1. 4:45 am · Brahma muhurta

    The city wakes before the sun

    Temple bells start in the dark. At Assi, Subah-e-Banaras begins with Vedic chants, a morning raga and the aarti, while the sky turns from indigo to rose.

  2. 6:15 am · Sunrise

    On the river, past the ghats

    A wooden boat drifts downstream from Assi. Pilgrims bathe, the dhobis begin their washing, and the first light turns the sandstone gold.

  3. 8:30 am · Nashta

    Kachori, sabzi and hot jalebi

    Breakfast is eaten standing up in the galis: puffed kachoris with spicy aloo, jalebi straight from the kadhai, and a kulhad of chai to finish.

  4. 11:00 am · At the loom

    The clack of the handloom

    In Madanpura and Sarai Mohana, the rhythm of the looms carries into the lanes. Sit with a weaver while a single motif grows, one thread at a time.

  5. 3:00 pm · Sarnath

    Ten kilometres, and a world away

    Drive out to Sarnath, where the Buddha gave his first sermon. Sit under the trees by the Dhamek Stupa while the afternoon goes quiet.

  6. 5:30 pm · Godhuli

    Chaat in the evening rush

    As the light softens, Godowlia fills up. Tamatar chaat in a leaf bowl, sweet, sour and smoky, is how Banaras says the working day is done.

  7. After sunset · Aarti

    Fire on the water

    Bells, conch shells and great brass lamps at Dashashwamedh. The aarti begins soon after sunset, which today in Banaras is at dusk.

  8. 9:00 pm · Baithak

    Ragas on a rooftop

    A small gathering, a sitar and a tabla, the river dark below. Music is how Banaras ends its day, as it has for centuries.

  9. 10:30 pm · Paan

    A Banarasi paan to close the day

    A betel leaf folded around gulkand, fennel and a little katha. Every Banarasi has their own paanwala, and no evening is complete without a stop there.

The motifs

Read a saree
the way a weaver does.

  • Butti बूटी

    Small flowers or leaves scattered across the body of the saree. The smaller and closer they sit, the more hours on the loom.

    See sarees with Butti →

  • Jaal जाल

    A net of vines and flowers that covers the whole saree, leaving almost no plain silk. Usually kept for the richest bridal pieces.

    See sarees with Jaal →

  • Bel बेल

    A running creeper along the border or the pallu. It stands for continuity and growth, which is why it so often frames a bridal saree.

    See sarees with Bel →

  • Kalga कलगा

    The paisley, shaped like a mango or a flame bending in the wind. Woven alone as a large motif, or in rows across the pallu.

    See sarees with Kalga →

  • Konia कोनिया

    A motif tucked into the corner where the border meets the pallu. Small and easy to miss, and a sign of a careful weaver.

    See sarees with Konia →

  • Shikargah शिकारगाह

    Hunting scenes of deer, horses and riders among trees. An old courtly design that only a few master weavers still take on.

At the loom

The language of the loom

Four words every
Banarasi lover knows.

Kadwa
Each motif woven separately by hand. Turn the saree over and there are no loose threads: the surest sign of a true handloom.
Meenakari
Coloured silk threads set into the gold like enamel on jewellery.
Tanchoi
Dense, layered patterns woven with several coloured wefts at once.
Brocade
Raised gold and silver zari motifs, the signature of Banaras for centuries.

Banarasi Khana

A city
you can taste.

Banaras eats by the season and by the hour. Here's what we'd put in front of you, and when.

Read the full Varanasi food guide →

In season now in Banaras: kachori, chaat and paan, as always.

  • Malaiyo

    मलइयोNov – Feb

    Milk left out under the winter dew, then whisked into a cloud with saffron and pistachio. Gone by mid-morning, and made only in the cold months.

  • Kachori & jalebi

    कचौड़ी-जलेबीEvery morning

    The Banarasi breakfast: puffed kachoris with a spicy potato sabzi, then jalebi hot from the kadhai.

  • Tamatar chaat

    टमाटर चाटEvery evening

    Tomatoes cooked down with potato, ginger and spices, topped with crushed namak-para and a sweet syrup. You won't find it like this anywhere else.

  • Choora matar

    चूड़ा मटरWinter

    Flattened rice tossed with fresh green peas, ghee and a little sugar, when the winter peas are at their sweetest.

  • Launglata

    लौंगलताAll year

    A crisp pastry filled with khoya, sealed shut with a single clove and soaked in syrup.

  • Rabri

    रबड़ीAll year

    Milk simmered for hours until it gathers into soft layers of cream, served cold in a kulhad.

  • Thandai

    ठंडाईHoli to summer

    Chilled milk ground with almonds, fennel, rose and black pepper. Poured by the bucket at Holi and Mahashivratri.

  • Kulhad lassi

    कुल्हड़ लस्सीThe warm months

    Thick curd churned by hand, topped with a layer of malai, served in a clay cup that goes back to the earth.

  • Langda aam

    लंगड़ा आमJune – July

    Banaras's own mango, still green-skinned when ripe, sweet and almost fibreless. It carries a GI tag.

  • Banarasi paan

    बनारसी पानAfter every meal

    Betel leaf folded with gulkand, fennel and katha. It carries a GI tag, and the city has a paanwala on every corner.

Sarnath · सारनाथ

Where the wheel
first turned.

About ten kilometres from the ghats, in a deer park then called Isipatana, the Buddha gave his first sermon to five companions. It set the Wheel of Dharma turning, and pilgrims have come here ever since.

Dhamek Stupa
A great stupa of brick and carved stone marking the place of the first sermon. Its present form dates from around the 5th–6th century. View on map ↗
The Lion Capital
Four lions back to back, carved in polished sandstone for Emperor Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE. It is India's national emblem, and you can stand before the original in the Sarnath Museum. View on map ↗
Mulagandha Kuti Vihara
A temple consecrated in 1931, its walls painted with the life of the Buddha by the Japanese artist Kosetsu Nosu. View on map ↗
The monasteries
Thai, Tibetan, Japanese, Chinese and Burmese temples, each in its own style, a short walk from one another.

Sawasdee · Yōkoso · Hwanyeong · Āyubōwan · Tashi delek

We welcome pilgrims from Thailand, Japan, Korea, Sri Lanka, Bhutan and beyond, and arrange monastery visits, meditation sessions and guides who know the sutras.

Voices of Banaras

The city that
shaped them.

  • 16th century

    Tulsidas

    Is said to have written much of the Ramcharitmanas at the ghat that carries his name. The Ramlila of Ramnagar still follows his verses.

  • 1827

    Mirza Ghalib

    Passed through on his way to Calcutta and wrote Chiragh-e-Dair, “The Lamp of the Temple”, a Persian poem in praise of Banaras.

  • 1880 – 1936

    Munshi Premchand

    Born in Lamhi, a village just outside the city, he wrote the lives of ordinary people into Hindi and Urdu literature.

  • 1916 – 2006

    Ustad Bismillah Khan

    Played his shehnai at the temples and ghats of Banaras for decades, and never agreed to leave the city, or its Ganga, for anywhere else.

  • 1920 – 2012

    Pandit Ravi Shankar

    Born in Banaras, he carried the sitar and the ragas of India to concert halls around the world.

  • 1929 – 2017

    Girija Devi

    The “Queen of Thumri”, who took the Banaras gharana's songs of longing and monsoon to stages everywhere.

Buying guide

Pure silk or synthetic? How to tell

Feel it first: pure silk warms to the touch and has a soft, dry rustle. Look for the Silk Mark tag. Then the thread burn test: real silk smells like burnt hair and leaves a fine ash that crumbles, while polyester melts into a hard bead and smells of plastic.

Care

Caring for a heritage handloom

Dry-clean only. Wrap it in soft muslin, never plastic. Refold it along new lines every few months so the zari doesn't crack, and keep it away from perfume and direct sun. Looked after this way, a Banarasi saree can be handed down to the next generation.

Travel

When to visit Banaras

October to March is the most pleasant, with misty winter mornings and malaiyo in the lanes. Dev Deepawali and Mahashivratri are unforgettable, so write to us early for both. Summers are hot, but bring mangoes, lassi and the Sankat Mochan music festival.

Bridal

Commissioning a bridal saree

A made-to-order bridal Katan usually needs several weeks on the loom. Share your colours, motifs and date with us, and we'll sit with the weaver to design it with you over video call.

Etiquette

Visiting the ghats and temples

Dress modestly and take your shoes off where others do. Ask before photographing people, and never photograph at Manikarnika or Harishchandra, the cremation ghats. Phones and bags aren't allowed inside Kashi Vishwanath, so we'll arrange a locker.

Travel

Getting around the old city

The galis are too narrow for cars, so the best way is on foot, with an e-rickshaw to the edge of the old city. The river is the easiest road of all: a boat between ghats is faster than any lane.
