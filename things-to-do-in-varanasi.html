Things to Do in Varanasi: A Local's Guide to Kashi and Sarnath

Varanasi rewards the slow traveller. These are the experiences we'd plan for family visiting us, in the order the city itself keeps them, from before dawn to late at night.

1. Take a boat on the Ganga at sunrise

Be on the river before the sun comes up. A wooden rowing boat from Assi Ghat drifts north past the ghats as pilgrims bathe, priests begin their rituals and the first light turns the sandstone gold. It's the single most memorable hour in Varanasi.

2. Start the day with Subah-e-Banaras at Assi Ghat

Every morning at Assi Ghat, Subah-e-Banaras begins in the dark with Vedic chanting, a morning aarti, a classical raga and yoga. Arrive early for a seat on the steps.

3. Watch the evening Ganga aarti

The evening aarti at Dashashwamedh Ghat begins soon after sunset: priests in saffron move great brass lamps in unison to bells and conch shells. Arrive early, or watch from a boat on the river for a calmer view.

4. Visit Kashi Vishwanath and Kal Bhairav

Kashi Vishwanath, one of the twelve Jyotirlingas of Shiva, is the spiritual centre of the city. Phones and bags aren't allowed inside, so plan for a locker. Many visitors also pay respects at Kal Bhairav, the city's guardian deity.

5. Walk the old-city galis, and eat as you go

The lanes of the old city, around Chowk and Godowlia, are too narrow for cars, and that's the joy of them. Breakfast on kachori-sabzi and hot jalebi, try tamatar chaat in the evening, and end with a Banarasi paan. Our Varanasi food guide lists what to eat and when.

6. Sit with a weaver at the loom

Banarasi silk is still woven by hand in neighbourhoods like Madanpura, Sarai Mohana and Lohta. Watching a motif grow thread by thread on a pit loom is unforgettable, and it's the best way to understand what makes a real Banarasi.

7. Spend a quiet afternoon in Sarnath

About ten kilometres from the ghats, Sarnath is where the Buddha gave his first sermon. See the Dhamek Stupa, the Ashoka Lion Capital (India's national emblem) in the Sarnath Museum, the Mulagandha Kuti Vihara with its Japanese murals, and the Thai, Tibetan, Japanese and Chinese monasteries. Go in the afternoon, when it's peaceful.

8. Hear the music of the Banaras gharana

Banaras gave India Ustad Bismillah Khan, Girija Devi and Pandit Ravi Shankar. An intimate evening baithak of sitar, tabla or thumri, ideally on a rooftop above the river, is how the city ends its day.

When to visit Varanasi

  • October to March is the most pleasant: cool, clear days and misty winter mornings.
  • Dev Deepawali (November) and Mahashivratri (February–March) are spectacular. Book well ahead.
  • April to June is very hot, but brings mangoes, lassi and the Sankat Mochan music festival.
  • July to September is monsoon; the river rises and some ghats go under water.

Etiquette at the ghats and temples

  • Dress modestly and remove your shoes where others do.
  • Ask before photographing people, especially sadhus and pilgrims.
  • Never photograph at the cremation ghats, Manikarnika and Harishchandra.
  • Agree on boat and rickshaw prices before you set off, or travel with a local host.

Questions people ask

How many days do you need in Varanasi?

Two to three days lets you see the sunrise boat, the evening aarti, Kashi Vishwanath, the old-city lanes and Sarnath without rushing. Four or five days lets you add a weaver visit, music and slower mornings on the ghats.

What time is the Ganga aarti in Varanasi?

The evening Ganga aarti at Dashashwamedh Ghat begins soon after sunset, so the time shifts through the year, roughly from about 6 pm in winter to around 7 pm in summer. Arrive 45 minutes early for a good spot.

How far is Sarnath from Varanasi?

Sarnath is about ten kilometres north-east of the ghats of Varanasi, usually 30 to 45 minutes by road depending on traffic.

What is the best month to visit Varanasi?

October to March is the most comfortable, with November especially popular for Dev Deepawali, when the ghats are lit with lakhs of diyas.
