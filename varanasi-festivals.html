Festivals of Varanasi: The Year in Celebrations, Month by Month

Banaras celebrates something almost every week. These are the festivals worth planning a trip around. Dates follow the Hindu lunar calendar and change every year, so check the exact dates before you book.

February–March: Mahashivratri

Kashi is Shiva's own city, and this is his night. Processions wind through the lanes and devotees keep an all-night vigil at Kashi Vishwanath and temples across the city.

February–March: Dhrupad Mela at Tulsi Ghat

Several nights of dhrupad, India's oldest surviving form of classical music, sung on the stone steps of Tulsi Ghat.

March: Holi and Rangbhari Ekadashi

Holi begins early in Banaras. On Rangbhari Ekadashi, gulal flies at Kashi Vishwanath, and the colour then spills onto every ghat and lane in the days before Holi.

April: Sankat Mochan Sangeet Samaroh

For close to a week around Hanuman Jayanti, leading classical musicians and dancers perform through the night in the courtyard of the Sankat Mochan temple, free and open to all.

April–May: Buddha Purnima in Sarnath

Monks and pilgrims from across Asia gather at Sarnath to remember the Buddha's birth, enlightenment and passing.

May–June: Ganga Dussehra

The day the Ganga is said to have descended to earth. The ghats fill with bathers at dawn, and lamps float on the river at dusk.

September–October: Ramnagar Ramlila

Across the river in Ramnagar, the Ramayana is performed over about a month in open-air settings across the town, a tradition kept for nearly two centuries under the patronage of the Kashi Naresh. UNESCO lists the Ramlila among India's intangible cultural heritage.

October: Bharat Milap at Nati Imli

The day after Dussehra, huge crowds gather at Nati Imli to watch Ram embrace his brother Bharat after fourteen years of exile. It's one of the city's best-loved days.

October–November: Nag Nathaiya at Tulsi Ghat

For one evening in Kartik, the Ganga stands in for the Yamuna as young Krishna leaps in to subdue the serpent Kaliya, watched by packed ghats and boats.

November: Dev Deepawali

On Kartik Purnima, fifteen days after Diwali, the gods are said to come down to bathe in the Ganga, and the ghats are lit with lakhs of diyas. It is the most spectacular night of the year in Varanasi, and boats and riverside rooms are booked months in advance.

Questions people ask

When is Dev Deepawali in Varanasi?

Dev Deepawali falls on Kartik Purnima, the full moon about fifteen days after Diwali, usually in November. The exact date changes each year with the lunar calendar.

What is the best festival to see in Varanasi?

Dev Deepawali, when the ghats glow with lakhs of diyas, is the most spectacular. Mahashivratri, Holi and the Ramnagar Ramlila are also unforgettable.

Do I need to book early for festivals in Varanasi?

Yes. For Dev Deepawali and Mahashivratri, boats, riverside rooms and good viewing spots are often booked weeks or months ahead.
